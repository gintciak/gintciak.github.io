Accounting for Crew Self-Organization in Planning Well Intervention Measures in an Oil-Producing Region

Шарко П.А., Гинцяк А.М.

Bibliographic citation

Шарко П.А., Гинцяк А.М. Учёт самоорганизации бригад при планировании геолого-технических мероприятий в регионе нефтедобычи // Управление инновациями в условиях цифровой трансформации: сборник трудов V Всероссийской научно-практической конференции. 2026. С. 278–283.

Conference proceedings

Research problem

The outcome of a schedule for work on oil wells depends partly on how individual crews plan and follow their routes. The paper investigates how to represent this capacity for local adaptation in a production scheduling model.

Approach and methods

The oil-producing region forms the environment of a multi-agent model and the crews are represented as cognitive agents. The implementation uses a belief, desire and intention architecture. Agent plans encode routing and job selection rules. When selecting a well, a crew considers the expected production gain and whether the job can finish within the planning horizon.

Findings

The paper shows a schedule for 15 wells. In a separate 77-well scenario on data approximating real operations, estimates of output differed from the reference model by under 0.5%. The modeled increase relative to doing no interventions was about 4%. The authors report that alternative optimizers produced a schedule with better performance.

Practical relevance

The model supports comparisons of crew routing rules and assessment of their collective consequences. The experiments show how to check modeled outcomes against a reference calculation and where the scheduling rules can be improved.

Research areas

  • Multi-agent systems
  • Digital modelling of production systems
  • Simulation-based optimization
