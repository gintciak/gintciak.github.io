Models and Methods for Managing Research Projects Using Game Theory

Гинцяк А.М.

Bibliographic citation

Publication pageFull text

Research problem

The dissertation treats an R&D project as an organizational system in which the funding organization, project leader and researchers make interdependent decisions. It develops models and methods for studying the expected outcomes of those decisions and assessing potential management interventions.

Approach and methods

An ontology of the project provides the basis for a general game-theoretic model. A method for deriving specific cases sets out how to identify participants, strategies and utilities, examine equilibrium profiles and compare them with optimal profiles. For selected problems, the analytical study is complemented by software implementations and simulation experiments.

Findings

Three case models address research team formation, participation in a project grant competition and the handling of exploratory tasks within a research team. The dissertation identifies conditions under which equilibrium and optimal outcomes differ and considers ways to change the rules of interaction. The models were applied to projects in two laboratories at Peter the Great St. Petersburg Polytechnic University.

Practical relevance

The work gives project leaders and grant organizers a basis for assessing team size, funding rules and the allocation of exploratory work. The dissertation provides the formal models, experimental results and case studies.
