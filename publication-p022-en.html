Intelligent Data Analysis for Infection Spread Prediction

Alexey I. Borovkov, Marina V. Bolsunovskaya, Aleksei M. Gintciak

Bibliographic citation

Alexey I. Borovkov, Marina V. Bolsunovskaya, Aleksei M. Gintciak. Intelligent Data Analysis for Infection Spread Prediction // Sustainability. 2022. Vol. 14. No. 4. Article 1995.

Publication pageDOIFull text

Research problem

Regional infection forecasting raises a question about the amount of detail needed in the input data. The study tests whether starting from individual records provides an advantage for a high-level system dynamics model of COVID-19 spread.

Approach and methods

The authors compare forecasts for St. Petersburg based on two datasets. One was collected and processed in aggregate form. The other was derived from individual records. Both forecasts use a modified susceptible–exposed–infected–recovered model with an additional group for people in quarantine, allowing the model to account for regional contact restrictions.

Findings

The results support the use of aggregated information for the type of model and experiment examined. This conclusion is tied to the level of aggregation and the model specification. The authors discuss how vaccination, reinfection and differences in population behaviour would affect the requirements for an extended model.

Practical relevance

The comparison can inform data collection for regional forecasting. Where a high-level model is adequate for a research question, using aggregated inputs reduces the need to process personal information.
