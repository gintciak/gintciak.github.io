Intelligent Decision Support System Based on Multi-Agent Interaction Models by the Example of the Oil and Gas Industry

Zhanna V. Burlutskaya, Polina A. Sharko, Aleksei M. Gintciak, Pavel A. Zakharov

Bibliographic citation

Zhanna V. Burlutskaya, Polina A. Sharko, Aleksei M. Gintciak, Pavel A. Zakharov. Intelligent Decision Support System Based on Multi-Agent Interaction Models by the Example of the Oil and Gas Industry // International Journal of Technology. 2026. Vol. 17. No. 2. pp. 344–356.

Publication pageDOIFull text

Research problem

Planning field development requires coordinating regional production targets with the constraints of individual operating sites. Information about wells, crews, and operations is distributed across levels of management. The paper studies how to incorporate that local information into a system that proposes schedules and crew assignments.

Approach and methods

The multi-agent model represents a region, its fields, groups of wells, and crews. Agent behavior is specified using a belief, desire, and intention (BDI) architecture. The agents interact with a Python simulation that estimates production, the effect of well interventions, production from drilling, and costs. The authors compare alternative optimization methods using the same simulation environment.

Findings

Testing used synthetic data designed to reflect operating conditions. The modeled region included four fields and 348 wells. In the experiment, the multi-agent approach increased forecast production by 4–4.5% and produced feasible field plans. The authors also identify scope for reducing operating costs by optimizing crew routes.

Practical relevance

The system provides a way to evaluate production performance and schedule feasibility together. Transfer to another region would require configuration of forecasting modules, the agent hierarchy, and data on operational constraints.
