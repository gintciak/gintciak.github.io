A Discrete-Event Model of a Parts Delivery System Using Transport Robots

Гаургов С.С., Пономарев А.В., Гладков Н.В., Гинцяк А.М.

Bibliographic citation

Гаургов С.С., Пономарев А.В., Гладков Н.В., Гинцяк А.М. Дискретно-событийная модель системы доставки деталей транспортерными роботами // Отходы и ресурсы. 2026. Т. 13. № 2. Статья 11INOR226.

Publication pageDOIFull text

Research problem

Sizing the fleet of transport robots for an assembly area requires balancing throughput, timely delivery and energy use. This paper studies those trade-offs for a network of routes between workstations in a manufacturing shop.

Approach and methods

The authors built an AnyLogic discrete-event model of an area with 11 workstations. It represents the route network, vehicle movement and loading constraints, energy use and the assignment of delivery jobs. The experiments vary line throughput and fleet size. Performance measures include vehicle utilization, schedule violation penalties and energy consumption.

Findings

For the modeled facility, one vehicle per route served throughput of up to 15 bodies per hour. Two vehicles limited delays at about 15 to 25 bodies per hour, and the simulations indicated a need for three at higher rates. At a fixed throughput of 20 bodies per hour, the estimated energy indicator rose from 35.0 to 35.8 conventional units as the fleet grew from one to three vehicles.

Practical relevance

The model supports comparing fleet and layout options before purchasing equipment or reconfiguring work areas. The throughput thresholds apply to the layout and operating conditions studied in the paper.
