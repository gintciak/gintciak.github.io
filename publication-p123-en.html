Organizational Systems Control Model with Bounded Rationality of the Control Subjects

Kapiton N. Pospelov, Aleksei M. Gintciak

Bibliographic citation

Kapiton N. Pospelov, Aleksei M. Gintciak. Organizational Systems Control Model with Bounded Rationality of the Control Subjects // International Journal of Technology. 2026. Vol. 17. No. 5. pp. 1705–1718.

Publication pageDOIFull text

Research problem

Distributed decision makers receive information about the same system with varying accuracy. Their assessments depend on prior experience, communication and links within the organization that may be hidden from them. The authors ask how to represent these influences when forecasting the consequences of managerial action.

Approach and methods

The state of the controlled system is described as a vector of attributes. A direct bounded rationality function gives a probabilistic mapping from actual to perceived states. Its parameters account for the precision of measurements and judgments, the relevance of past experience and aspects of communication. An inverse function uses Bayesian reasoning to estimate possible actual states from perceived ones.

Findings

The paper specifies both mappings and places them in a decision model. Illustrations show how changing the confidence level changes the size of the region of plausible states in attribute space. The paper formalizes an approach for predictive analysis, and further empirical work is required to validate its application to real systems.

Practical relevance

A decision support system could use this approach to explore the range of possible effects of a decision in an innovation project. Application requires current and retrospective observations, along with information about how decision makers assess the system's attributes.

Research areas

  • Intelligent decision support systems
  • Multi-agent systems
  • Project and portfolio management

Keywords

bounded rationality, organizational systems, state space, Bayesian inference, decision support
