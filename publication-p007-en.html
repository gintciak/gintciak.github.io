Comparative Analysis of Approaches to the Employees’ Distribution Among the Organization’s Projects

Gintciak A., Bolsunovskaya M., Redko S.

Bibliographic citation

Gintciak A., Bolsunovskaya M., Redko S. Comparative Analysis of Approaches to the Employees’ Distribution Among the Organization’s Projects // Proceedings of the 33rd International Business Information Management Association Conference, IBIMA 2019: Education Excellence and Innovation Management through Vision 2020. 2019. pp. 4690–4694.

Publication page

Research problem

A project-oriented organisation assigns employees both to deliver current work and to build skills for future projects. This review examines how staffing models represent those goals and why a decision that serves one project may differ from a decision made for the organisation as a whole.

Approach and methods

The authors compare approaches aimed at local optimisation with models concerned with developing human potential. The first group includes staff scheduling, the use of employees across several projects, and team formation based on participant roles. The second group includes the assignment of experienced and junior professionals to support knowledge transfer.

Findings

The authors make a case for an objective centred on long-term skill development subject to project requirements. Their analysis of published formulations also identifies assumptions about objective weights, deadlines and budgets that need to be examined before applying a model.

Practical relevance

The review helps define a staffing problem at portfolio level. It gives researchers and managers a rationale for making competency development an explicit objective while preserving commitments to work that is already scheduled.

Research areas

  • Project and portfolio management
  • Multi-objective and stochastic optimization

Keywords

personnel allocation, project-oriented organisation, competency development, project teams
