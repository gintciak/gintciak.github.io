COVID-19 Spread Modeling Considering Vaccination and Re-Morbidity

Aleksey I. Borovkov, Marina V. Bolsunovskaya, Aleksei M. Gintciak, Valeriya V. Rakova, Marina O. Efremova, Ruslan B. Akbarov

Bibliographic citation

Aleksey I. Borovkov, Marina V. Bolsunovskaya, Aleksei M. Gintciak, Valeriya V. Rakova, Marina O. Efremova, Ruslan B. Akbarov. COVID-19 Spread Modeling Considering Vaccination and Re-Morbidity // International Journal of Technology. 2022. Vol. 13. No. 7. pp. 1463–1472.

Publication pageDOIFull text

Research problem

The study concerns forecasts in a setting where vaccination and repeat infections are possible. It tests whether representing those processes improves forecasting performance relative to a previously published system dynamics model.

Approach and methods

The authors develop an extension of the susceptible–exposed–infected–recovered model called SVEIRS. It represents transitions associated with vaccination, recovery and the return to susceptibility after immunity wanes. The new model is compared with the SEVIS reference model using data for Moscow. Thirty days are used for calibration and the subsequent 60 days for forecast evaluation.

Findings

The mean absolute percentage error on the evaluation period was 3.329% for SVEIRS and 6.309% for SEVIS. Calibration accuracy was comparable. The result covers a single region and period in 2022. The paper calls for testing in additional settings and discusses assumptions about virus variants and the development of immunity.

Practical relevance

The model provides a basis for regional scenario studies. Evaluation on a separate period helps assess how the model structure affects forecasts before applying it to new data.
