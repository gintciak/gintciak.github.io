Developing Competency Profiles of IT Specialists Based on Semantic Analysis of Vacancies

Aleksey Gintciak, Andrey Rytik, Denis Terin, Marina Kudaka, Dmitriy Kaisyn, Aleksey Ignatev, Aleksey Marakhtanov, Oksana Gladkih

Bibliographic citation

Aleksey Gintciak, Andrey Rytik, Denis Terin, Marina Kudaka, Dmitriy Kaisyn, Aleksey Ignatev, Aleksey Marakhtanov, Oksana Gladkih. Developing Competency Profiles of IT Specialists Based on Semantic Analysis of Vacancies // Journal of Physics: Conference Series. IOP Publishing. 2019. Vol. 1405. No. 1. Article 012003.

Publication pageDOIFull text

Research problem

The study asks how job advertisements can reveal which combinations of skills employers expect from IT specialists. A competency map derived from those texts can show differences among specialisations and supplement expert descriptions of labour market requirements.

Approach and methods

The authors select 26,450 records from the Work in Russia vacancy database. After cleaning the data, they represent recurring requirement phrases as vertices in a weighted graph. The weight of an edge is the number of advertisements in which two requirements occur together. They detect communities using the Louvain method and examine general and specialist skills separately.

Findings

The resulting graph contains 20,257 vertices and 106,985 edges. The authors interpret its modularity of 0.895 as evidence of distinct groups of competencies. A web development cluster illustrates the interpretation. The paper also discusses the effect that incomplete data cleaning can have on the number of detected groups.

Practical relevance

The method can inform professional standards and programme design. Comparing maps made from vacancies and résumés is proposed as a further step towards measuring gaps between the demand for skills and their supply.
