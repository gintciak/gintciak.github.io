Applying a Multi-Agent Approach to Scheduling in Distributed Production Systems

Гинцяк А.М., Бурлуцкая Ж.В., Поспелов К.Н., Ватаманюк И.В.

Research problem

A distributed production schedule has to account for the interests of several decision centres. Units share resources, work with incomplete information and evaluate decisions using different measures. A formally feasible plan may therefore meet resistance from participants and require revisions during execution.

Approach and methods

The authors relate practical scheduling problems to properties of distributed production. They examine autonomous actors, vertical and horizontal links, shared resources, differences in objective functions, competition and cooperation. They consider how to identify the agents and formalize their objectives, interactions and organizational constraints.

Findings

The paper identifies features that help determine when a multi-agent model is relevant. It proposes that simplified representations of decision makers can contribute to unstable schedules. The authors outline the representation of active agents that observe changes in their environment and respond to them. The study establishes a problem formulation for further work.

Practical relevance

The list of features can guide an assessment of a production system before a scheduling model is developed. It helps determine which participants to represent and which objectives, resources and relationships should be taken into account when evaluating candidate schedules.

Research areas
