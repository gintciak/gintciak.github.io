Simulation Modelling Application for Balancing Epidemic and Economic Crisis in the Region

Aleksey I. Borovkov, Marina V. Bolsunovskaya, Aleksei M. Gintciak, Tatiana Ju. Kudryavtseva

Bibliographic citation

Aleksey I. Borovkov, Marina V. Bolsunovskaya, Aleksei M. Gintciak, Tatiana Ju. Kudryavtseva. Simulation Modelling Application for Balancing Epidemic and Economic Crisis in the Region // International Journal of Technology. 2020. Vol. 11. No. 8. pp. 1579–1588.

Publication pageDOIFull text

Research problem

This paper examines the consequences of temporarily restricting activity in different sectors during an epidemic. A regional model needs to account for both the trajectory of infections and the economic burden associated with time that employees spend in isolation.

Approach and methods

The authors extend a susceptible–exposed–infected–recovered model with a separate group for individuals in isolation. Scenarios specify which sectors are affected and when restrictions are introduced and lifted. Six simulation experiments for St. Petersburg compare peak and cumulative infections, the time taken to fall below a prevalence threshold, and person-days spent in isolation.

Findings

In the scenarios examined, earlier interventions had a greater impact on the epidemic indicators. The model connects the timing and coverage of restrictions with different infection trajectories and amounts of lost working time. The authors discuss their assumptions about workplace contacts and the representation of restrictions by sector.

Practical relevance

The model provides a basis for comparing regional policy scenarios. Its indicators allow decision-makers to consider the potential burden on healthcare and the economic effects together under the assumptions used in the experiment.
