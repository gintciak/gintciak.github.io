Hybrid Digital Models Using Artificial Intelligence Technologies

Бекетов С.М., Хабибуллина Л.М., Поспелов К.Н., Нигматулин А.Д., Васильев Г.В., Гинцяк А.М.

Bibliographic citation

Full texteLIBRARY record

Research problem

Digital models of complex systems may combine mechanistic equations, symbolic rules, and algorithms trained on data. The paper examines the ways in which these components can be connected. It considers how integration choices affect the interpretability, adaptability, and computational properties of the resulting model.

Approach and methods

The study combines a systematic literature review with an analysis of publication activity. It compares work on neural networks, surrogate modeling, digital twins, and reinforcement learning. Approaches are classified by the depth of integration, the direction in which information passes between the model and the artificial intelligence component, and the type of technology used.

Findings

The authors propose a multilevel classification and describe the problems addressed by its categories. The review identifies growing interest in two-way links between modeling and learning, explainable methods, and adaptive digital twins. The classification describes the architectures encountered in the literature and can be extended as the field develops.

Practical relevance

The review helps researchers compare hybrid architectures when developing a digital model. Relevant choices depend on the data available, the required interpretability, and the kind of uncertainty being represented. The classification offers a basis for further comparisons in applied research.

Research areas

  • Intelligent decision support systems
  • Digital modelling of production systems
