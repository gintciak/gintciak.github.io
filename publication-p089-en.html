Integrating Simulation Modelling and Game Theory to Study Interaction Strategies of Patent Law Participants

Лундаева К.А., Гинцяк А.М.

DOILibrary recordConference proceedings

Research problem

Strategies for protecting and using intellectual property can change as participants learn more about judicial practice and enforcement. The paper develops a hybrid modelling framework for moving from the analysis of a one-time interaction between a rights holder and another party to the study of decisions repeated over time.

Approach and methods

Game theory is used to specify players, strategies and utilities and to solve a one-shot sequential game. Agent-based modelling describes changes in behaviour and conditions between iterations. A discrete-event component represents the sequence of decisions, outcomes and delays. In the proposed framework, estimates of the probability of successful protection are updated from earlier interactions and feed back into the agents' utilities.

Findings

The paper presents a conceptual hybrid model and separate algorithms for the game-theoretic, agent-based and discrete-event stages. It specifies which information moves between those stages, how events correspond to nodes of a game tree and how payoffs are recalculated. Software implementation is identified as future work.

Practical relevance

The approach can guide the design of simulation experiments comparing ways of regulating and protecting patent rights. It can help determine which scenarios to evaluate and what changes in strategies and payoffs to record.

Research areas

  • Game theory and strategic interaction
  • Simulation-based optimization
