Classification of the Game Theory Models in Project Management: Literature Review

Гинцяк А.

Bibliographic citation

Гинцяк А. Классификация теоретико-игровых моделей в управлении проектами: литературный обзор // Прикладная математика и вопросы управления. 2021. № 3. С. 130–153.

Publication pageDOIFull text

Research problem

Which game models can represent interactions among clients, contractors and team members at different stages of a project? This review examines how published studies express the interests of the parties, the choices available to them and the distribution of payoffs.

Approach and methods

Work from peer-reviewed journals and conference proceedings is classified by the participating parties and by game type. The first dimension distinguishes situations such as public–private partnerships, contractor and subcontractor relations, and interactions within teams. The second considers the order of moves, the possibility of cooperation, and whether the payoffs in a non-cooperative game have a constant sum.

Findings

Contracting problems account for much of the reviewed literature. Public–private partnerships were commonly represented by non-zero-sum games. Interactions among subcontractors often involved cooperative formulations where contract terms are agreed together. Informal interactions within project teams received less attention.

Practical relevance

The classification helps researchers select a game model for a specific problem. The paper also identifies scope for a framework that can represent projects as groups of connected strategic decisions across the project life cycle.

Research areas

  • Game theory and strategic interaction
  • Project and portfolio management
  • Intelligent decision support systems

Keywords

game theory, project management, strategic interactions, contract design, cooperative games
