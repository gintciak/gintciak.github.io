Operations Research in Innovation Process Management

Гинцяк А.М.

Research problem

This study guide for master's students in innovation management introduces mathematical modelling of complex organizational and technical systems. It focuses on two recurring decisions. One is how to choose a course of action when resources are limited. The other is how to estimate the completion time of a project containing activities that depend on one another and may run in parallel.

Approach and methods

The guide introduces the stages of model development and explains how to express a linear optimization problem through decision variables, an objective and constraints. It covers graphical solution, the simplex method and spreadsheet-based optimization. The scheduling chapters explain how to represent activities as a network or in tabular form. They cover the critical path method and the Program Evaluation and Review Technique (PERT) for uncertain activity durations.

Findings

Worked examples show how to translate a management problem into a mathematical formulation, identify feasible solutions and interpret an optimum. The scheduling examples cover early and late dates, float and the effect of activity-duration estimates on the expected completion date.

Practical relevance

The book supports a course on mathematical models of technical objects of control. Exercises and examination questions can be used for independent study and for practice in choosing a model and interpreting its output.

Research areas

  • Multi-objective and stochastic optimization
  • Project and portfolio management
  • Intelligent decision support systems

Keywords
