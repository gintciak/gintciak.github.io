Research problem

This master's study guide examines management situations in which each participant's outcome depends on decisions made by other stakeholders. It explains how to identify the players, assign possible strategies, specify when decisions are made and express the interests of participants through payoffs in a model of an innovation system.

Approach and methods

The book covers simultaneous games in normal form and sequential games in extensive form. It introduces dominance, elimination of dominated strategies, Nash equilibrium in pure and mixed strategies, and subgame-perfect equilibrium. Worked examples include the prisoner's dilemma, spatial competition, and the Cournot and Stackelberg models. A separate chapter examines open and sealed bidding, including first-price and second-price auctions.

Findings

The examples connect the structure of an interaction with the choice of solution method. Whether players can observe earlier moves or commit to a course of action affects how the game is analysed. The auction chapter provides a common framework for comparing rules about submitting bids and deciding how much the winner pays.

Practical relevance

The guide supports seminars on representing a decision situation as a payoff matrix or a game tree, calculating an equilibrium and interpreting its implications. It also includes questions for examination preparation.

Research areas
