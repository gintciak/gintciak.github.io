Demand Forecasting Model for Airline Flights Based on Historical Passenger Flow Data

Karina A. Lundaeva, Zakhar A. Saranin, Kapiton N. Pospelov, Aleksei M. Gintciak

Bibliographic citation

Karina A. Lundaeva, Zakhar A. Saranin, Kapiton N. Pospelov, Aleksei M. Gintciak. Demand Forecasting Model for Airline Flights Based on Historical Passenger Flow Data // Applied Sciences. 2024. Vol. 14. No. 23. Article 11413.

Publication pageDOIFull text

Research problem

An airline building a medium-term plan needs to estimate demand by route and departure time. This study asks what patterns can be recovered from flight and passenger-demand histories when demand changes with the season and with economic conditions in the departure and destination regions.

Approach and methods

The authors use the Prophet time-series model and multiple linear regression. They estimate seasonality from historical observations and consider gross regional product, median income per capita and population as external variables. The model is evaluated on one turnaround route. Demand is modelled separately in each direction. The reported accuracy comparison uses forecasts for 2023 from a model trained on data collected between 2019 and 2022.

Findings

The mean absolute percentage errors in the two directions are 35.705% and 31.435%. The authors treat this level of accuracy as a baseline for improvement. The paper also identifies dependence on the completeness of historical data, limited evidence about competitors and difficulties in forecasting demand for new routes.

Practical relevance

Forecasts of this kind can inform an initial comparison of timetables and associated resource requirements. For decisions sensitive to demand errors, the model's accuracy on the relevant route and the quality of its available input data are important considerations.
