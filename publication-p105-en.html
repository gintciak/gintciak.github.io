On the Problem of Forming Sustainable Production Schedules in the Context of Conflicting Objective Functions of Management Agents

Burlutskaya Z.V., Vatamaniuk I.V., Gintciak A.M., Ablavatskaya D.A., Pospelov K.N.

Publication pageDOIFull text

Research problem

A production schedule assigns work to units with different responsibilities and performance measures. A plan that looks favorable at the system level may produce losses for individual participants and prove difficult to implement. The paper examines how to organize the objectives and possible conflicts that a robust scheduling method needs to account for.

Approach and methods

The authors identify participants such as enterprises, departments, workers, suppliers, and customers. Their objectives are classified by the nature of the constraint and performance measure, level of detail, behavioral orientation, and dependence on decisions made elsewhere in the system. For different pairs of participants, the paper relates patterns of interaction to suitable classes of game-theoretic models.

Findings

The main results are a taxonomy of objective functions and a mapping from typical interactions to game models. The analysis shows how conflicts over deadlines, quality, utilization, and costs can emerge during schedule coordination. The taxonomy derives requirements for future approaches to testing schedule robustness from the properties of individual agents.

Practical relevance

The results support the formulation of production planning models. They can help identify whose constraints need to be checked, which objectives might conflict, and where a decision support system should represent communication or strategic behavior.

Research areas
