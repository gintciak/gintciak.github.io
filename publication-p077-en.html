Team Formation in Software Projects: Multi-criteria Pareto Optimization

S. M. Beketov, M. V. Dergachev, A. M. Gintciak, S. G. Redko

Bibliographic citation

S. M. Beketov, M. V. Dergachev, A. M. Gintciak, S. G. Redko. Team Formation in Software Projects: Multi-criteria Pareto Optimization // Programmnaya Ingeneria. 2025. Vol. 16. No. 2. pp. 92–99.

Publication pageDOI

Research problem

Staffing a software development project involves selecting a team across several roles. Team size affects how long tasks take and how much labour they consume. Larger teams can also generate more coordination work. A manager therefore needs to consider both project objectives alongside the actual structure of tasks.

Approach and methods

The authors build a mathematical model and an algorithm that link team composition to a project network schedule. The model incorporates nominal productivity, the number of team members in each role and a parameter for the labour intensity of communication. For each feasible team, it estimates project duration and labour requirements and identifies the nondominated options.

Findings

The example project has analysts, developers and testers, with up to five people per role. Complete enumeration of 125 team configurations produces a Pareto front in the duration and labour plane. Each point is associated with a specific team composition.

Practical relevance

Project managers can use the set to shortlist staffing decisions before a project starts. They can then consider factors outside the two modelled criteria, such as individual availability, commitments to contractors and demand for the same specialists on other projects.
