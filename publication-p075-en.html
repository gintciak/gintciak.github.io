Developing an Agile Project Management System with an Intellectual Module for Project Duration Prediction

Denisov N., Gintciak A., Burlutskaya Z., Fedyaevskaya D., Beketov S.

Bibliographic citation

Denisov N., Gintciak A., Burlutskaya Z., Fedyaevskaya D., Beketov S. Developing an Agile Project Management System with an Intellectual Module for Project Duration Prediction // Sustainable development and engineering economics. 2024. Vol. 3. pp. 8–27.

Publication pageDOIFull text

Research problem

In agile projects, the scope of work can change as requirements are refined. Managers need to forecast both task and project duration, revise estimates when the backlog changes and work with a limited history when a team is newly formed.

Approach and methods

The method combines expert estimates of relative task effort in story points with Monte Carlo simulation. A distribution estimated from completed user stories supplies inputs for repeated computations. The model produces median, optimistic and pessimistic duration estimates. The authors incorporate an algorithm for updating these estimates into the architecture of a task and product backlog management system.

Findings

The method was evaluated using data from 15 software projects. The mean magnitude of relative error was about 41% across the full sample and about 23% for the subset of nine projects with at least 20 tasks available for training. The paper discusses sensitivity to a small number of completed tasks and to the quality of expert story-point estimates.

Practical relevance

Updating estimates when project scope changes can support decisions about upcoming sprints and help track the risk of missing a deadline. The system gives managers and customers both an expected duration and a range to discuss.
