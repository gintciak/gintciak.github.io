Formalization of the Scheduling Process in Distributed Production Systems as a Set of Strategic Interactions in Terms of Game Theory and Multi-Agent Systems

Аблавацкая Д.А., Бурлуцкая Ж.В., Шарко П.А., Поспелов К.Н., Гинцяк А.М.

Research problem

Scheduling in distributed production requires coordination among participants with different responsibilities, information, and performance measures. The paper examines how to formalize such a planning problem so that the effect of local actions on the feasibility of the overall schedule can be studied.

Approach and methods

The participants are classified by functional roles and position in the hierarchy. For key agents, the paper analyzes how their objectives are related, what actions are available, and which strategic interactions may occur. Agents are described using a belief, desire, and intention (BDI) architecture. Actions are expressed as strategies, and preferences and objectives are represented by utility functions.

Findings

The results include classifications of stakeholders and relationships among their objectives. The authors formulate general utility functions and a structure of strategic interactions for production planning models. The resulting framework specifies what needs to be represented when developing models for individual production systems.

Practical relevance

The framework can help a model developer identify relevant participants and define appropriate assumptions about objectives, authority, and available information. It can also inform the design of multi-agent scheduling support for production systems with different organizational structures.
