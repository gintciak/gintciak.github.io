Automatic Calibration of Sociotechnical Systems Simulation Models on The Example of The Infection Spread Model

Daria A. Zubkova, Valeriya V. Rakova, Zhanna V. Burlutskaya, Aleksei M. Gintciak

Bibliographic citation

Daria A. Zubkova, Valeriya V. Rakova, Zhanna V. Burlutskaya, Aleksei M. Gintciak. Automatic Calibration of Sociotechnical Systems Simulation Models on The Example of The Infection Spread Model // International Journal of Technology. 2022. Vol. 13. No. 7. pp. 1452–1462.

Publication pageDOIFull text

Research problem

Model parameters can change as an epidemic evolves, so forecasts need to be updated as new observations come in. The study looks for a calibration procedure that respects admissible parameter values, fits the observed trajectory and can be run within a practical time budget.

Approach and methods

The calibration target is an extended SEIR model that accounts for isolation, vaccination and recurrent illness. The authors compared a bespoke search procedure with tools from SciPy and lmfit using observations from Moscow and St. Petersburg. They assessed the quality of the fit and the computation time. The implementation offers automatic and manual selection of time intervals corresponding to waves in the observations.

Findings

The authors selected the bounded least-squares method in lmfit. In the experiments, it aligned the simulated and observed series with a substantially shorter computation time than the bespoke search. The software estimates parameters separately for each interval and can rerun the estimation when new surveillance data arrive.

Practical relevance

Automated calibration helps keep forecasts current for public health planning. Applying it in another region requires local input series. Adapting it to a different model also requires redefining the parameters to estimate and the error function.
