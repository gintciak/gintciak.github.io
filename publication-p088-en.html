Applying Transient-Response Quality Criteria from Automatic Control Theory to Production Process Management

Рыбакова Ю.В., Гинцяк А.М.

Research problem

A production system may take time to settle after a change in workload or a control action. The paper examines how to assess that transition using observables such as equipment speed, inventory and the deviation of an operating parameter from its target.

Approach and methods

The authors consider automatic control criteria used to characterize transient responses, including settling time, overshoot, oscillation, stability and steady-state error. Illustrative examples include adjusting conveyor speed after a change in the incoming flow of goods and using feedback for raw material inventory control.

Findings

The paper explains how to interpret the criteria in continuous production. Settling time describes the rate at which an operating condition stabilizes. Overshoot captures a temporary excursion beyond the target, while steady-state error captures a deviation that persists. The contribution is methodological and the examples illustrate its interpretation.

Practical relevance

Transient-response assessment can supplement the usual performance indicators. It provides a way to compare how processes react to disturbances and to discuss what should matter when configuring control rules. For instance, the time required to reach the target can be considered together with the size of transient fluctuations and the remaining deviation.
