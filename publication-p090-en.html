Systemic Product Approach to Designing the Process of Students' Research Activities

Селедцова И.А., Редько С.Г., Гинцяк А.М., Бурлуцкая Ж.В.

Research problem

The study asks how universities can organize master's research as a coherent educational process. The preparation students receive depends on supervisors' expectations, learners' needs, and demand for relevant skills. Changes to individual activities therefore need to be considered alongside the program's learning objectives, organizational arrangements, and mechanisms for feedback.

Approach and methods

The authors combine product design with a view of education as an open, complex organizational system. They consider user requirements, nonlinear relationships between activities, adaptability, and the capacity of participating groups to organize their work. An educational product should have identifiable, measurable value and remain usable after its development. The paper relates this reasoning to research training for Innovation Studies and Quality Management students at Peter the Great St. Petersburg Polytechnic University.

Findings

The paper formulates six design principles related to coherence and value, adaptation, interdependence, emergence, openness, and measurable results. Examples include studying learner needs, mapping links between activities, collecting feedback regularly, and tracking learning outcomes to guide revisions.

Practical relevance

The framework helps program designers coordinate teaching materials and changes to how student research is supervised. The university case illustrates how the principles can inform a specific program. Selecting and systematizing tools for each design stage is identified as a direction for further work.
