The Impact of Patent Regulation Features on the Innovative Activities of Enterprises

Karina A. Lundaeva, Aleksei M. Gintciak

Bibliographic citation

Karina A. Lundaeva, Aleksei M. Gintciak. The Impact of Patent Regulation Features on the Innovative Activities of Enterprises // International Journal of Technology. 2025. Vol. 16. No. 4. pp. 1093–1103.

Publication pageDOIFull text

Research problem

The paper examines strategic choices made by an intellectual property holder and an enterprise seeking to use an existing innovation. The question is how the probability of detecting a violation and the corresponding penalty affect the incentives to obtain a license or imitate the innovation. The analysis makes these incentives explicit through the payoffs of both parties.

Approach and methods

The authors model the holder's choice among invention patents, utility model patents, and a scenario in which a smart contract records intellectual property rights. The other actor chooses how to use the innovation, including whether to license or imitate it. Payoff functions incorporate the returns and costs associated with developing and using intellectual property. The study calibrates the scenario with reference to Russian patent regulation as of 2024.

Findings

In the tested configuration, imitation is part of the follower's equilibrium strategy under low and moderate supervision. Licensing becomes more attractive under strong supervision because the expected consequences of infringement reduce the payoff from imitation. In the analyzed cases, the holder continues to select the smart contract scenario. That finding is conditional on the modeled payoff structure.

Practical relevance

The model supports comparisons of alternative enforcement scenarios and helps specify the conditions under which companies would have an economic incentive to license an innovation.
