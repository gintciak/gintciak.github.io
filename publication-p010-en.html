Publication pageDOI

Research problem

The paper concerns recordings where the presenter and the projection screen appear in the same frame. The system needs to recognise which slide is showing, replace the projected image with its digital original, and preserve the lecturer's image when the layers are combined.

Approach and methods

The implementation uses BRIEF and ORB keypoint descriptors to match features in recorded frames and presentation slides. A geometric transformation places the matching slide within the screen area. A separate mask identifies the presenter. The paper also describes colour correction, presentation conversion and several output formats.

Findings

The prototype generates a processed video, an Adobe Premiere project with separate tracks, and a metadata file containing slide positions for individual frames. For studio recordings the authors report at least 98.2% real-time recognition and classification accuracy for key objects. For a camera within 1.5 metres of the screen and a resolution of at least 2K, they report a screen-boundary measurement error of at most 1 centimetre.

Practical relevance

The method improves slide legibility and leaves editable tracks for subsequent review. It connects computer vision with the practical process of producing educational videos.
